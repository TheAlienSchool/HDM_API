The Richer Structure

Every sensory observation is a projection of a generative model too complex to be directly perceived.

Academic Register

The dodecahedron is a twelve-faced solid of pure symmetry. Every single face is a regular pentagon, and inside every pentagon, the diagonal lines relate to its sides by the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618. (To see this geometry in detail, wander into the Interactive Dodecahedron Explorer). But here, we focus on projection: when you shine a light on a wireframe dodecahedron, it casts a flat, two-dimensional shadow that shifts as the shape rotates.

Observable Behavior = Shadow(Richer Structure)

This relationship is represented as Observable Behavior = Shadow(Richer Structure). Mathematically, this describes a dimensional projection :: collapsing a 3D wireframe dodecahedron onto a 2D plane. In cognitive science, the 'Richer Structure' is the brain's internal 3D generative model, and the 'Shadow' is the flat, ambiguous sensory data hitting the retina. To make sense of the shadows, the brain performs an inverse projection, reconstructing the higher-dimensional shape from its lower-dimensional observations.

In cognitive science, this shadow theater represents how we perceive reality. The brain does not see the world directly; it acts as a simulator. It builds a three-dimensional model of the world (the "richer structure") to explain the flat, ambiguous sensory data (the "shadows") hitting our eyes. We never touch the three-dimensional source directly; we infer it from its projections. What we call perception is the process of finding the beautiful, coherent shape that makes the shadows make sense.
